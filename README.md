Which e-mail should a retailer send, and how far can a company trust its own test results? One real
randomized experiment (64,000 customers) is taken from analysis plan to rollout, then **22,743** real
headline tests are read as a testing programme. Analysis plans were committed before the results, and
CI checks every number below against the recorded results.

## Key results

- **Send the men's e-mail to everyone.** It adds **+$0.77 spend per customer [+0.48, +1.05]** over two
  weeks, about **$207 per 1,000** e-mailed, and beats the women's e-mail by $0.34 [+0.03, +0.65].
- **Targeting by segment does not help.** It looked better in the search, but on held-out customers it
  changes spend by −$0.01 [−$0.28, +$0.26].
- **A routine sample-ratio check catches broken randomization.** Not told the dates, it flags **70%** of
  tests in the maintainers' known broken months and **0.5%** outside them.
- **Trust a winning headline about halfway.** Only 47% of a winner's lift survives on fresh traffic, yet
  picking winners still beats a random headline by +20% click-through.

## How it works

| Step | Method | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Plan | Metrics, caps, comparisons and decision rule written before any outcome was read | [Hillstrom plan](./reports/hillstrom_analysis_plan.md) · [Upworthy plan](./reports/upworthy_analysis_plan.md) |
| Design and trust | Smallest detectable lift ($0.31, against $0.25 to break even); sample ratio p = 0.90; covariate balance | [01](./notebooks/01_email_test_checks.ipynb) |
| Read out and decide | Regression adjustment (CUPED idea), Holm across comparisons, bootstrap and Bayesian cross-checks, dollars per 1,000, holdback sizing | [02](./notebooks/02_email_test_readout.ipynb) |
| Who benefits | Segment search on one half of the customers, confirmed on the other | [03](./notebooks/03_email_test_targeting.ipynb) |
| Programme checks | Sample-ratio screening, power per test, false discovery rate | [04](./notebooks/04_programme_sample_ratio.ipynb) |
| Winner's curse | Split-sample re-measurement, empirical-Bayes shrinkage | [05](./notebooks/05_programme_winners.ipynb) |

## Appendix · StreamFlix: the full workflow on simulated data

The project's first version: a simulated 4-week homepage test on 100,000 trialists, with a planted effect
and a planted Android assignment bug, analysed through eight notebooks. Because the true answer is known,
it shows the methods work.

- **Ship the personalized homepage:** trial-to-paid conversion **+2.54pp** [+2.02, +3.06] (+12% relative),
  P(treatment better) above 99.99%, positive on every device (TV's interval includes zero).
- **The data-quality check earns its keep:** a sample-ratio test (p = 0.0036) traced to an Android
  assignment bug; excluding Android, the lift is still +2.32pp, so the call holds.
- **The pipeline recovers the truth:** true effect +2.29pp, inside the interval; across 300 simulated
  reruns the 95% interval covers the truth 95.3% of the time.
- **One guardrail needs a fix before rollout:** page load +29ms, so the ship is conditional on engineering follow-up.

## Data

- **Hillstrom e-mail test** (MineThatData, 2008): 64,000 customers randomized in equal thirds to a men's
  e-mail, a women's e-mail or none. Not redistributed; download link and checksum in [`data/README.md`](./data/README.md).
- **Upworthy Research Archive** (2013–2015, CC BY 4.0): every headline test, split by the maintainers into
  exploratory, confirmatory and holdout samples. Committed in compact form.
- **StreamFlix** (appendix): simulated, with a known truth; generated by [`src/data/simulate.py`](./src/data/simulate.py).

## Limits

- The 40% margin and $0.10 cost per e-mail are assumptions, and Hillstrom is widely analysed, so the plan
  fixes the analysis choices but cannot make it blind to the headline result.
- Upworthy measures clicks, not reading or sharing.
- Regression adjustment narrowed intervals by under 2% here; the StreamFlix appendix shows a case where it helps.
